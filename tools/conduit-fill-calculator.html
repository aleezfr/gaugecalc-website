How conduit fill is calculated

Every conduit has an inside cross-section area, and every wire has an outside area that includes its insulation. Conduit fill is the total area of the wires divided by the conduit's area. The NEC caps that ratio so the wires are not crushed when pulled and have room to shed heat. The calculator adds up the wire areas you enter, then tests each EMT trade size from 1/2 inch up to 4 inch and returns the first one that passes.

Worked examples

Example 1: nine #12 wires

Nine 12 AWG wires have a total area of 9 × 0.0133 = 0.1197 sq in. The 40% area of 1/2" EMT is 0.122 sq in, so it passes at 39.4% fill. Add a tenth wire and the total rises to 0.133 sq in, which is over the limit, so you move to 3/4".

Example 2: mixed sizes

Six #12 wires, one #12 ground and three #10 wires make seven #12 and three #10 in total: 7 × 0.0133 + 3 × 0.0211 = 0.1564 sq in. 1/2" EMT is too small (0.122). 3/4" has 0.213 sq in at 40%, so it fits at about 29% fill.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to count the equipment grounding conductor.
  • Using a wire area for THHN when the real wire is a thicker insulation such as XHHW.
  • Applying the 40% limit to a two-wire pull, where 31% applies.
  • Choosing the exact minimum size when the run has many bends. Going up one trade size makes the pull easier.
  • Mixing up fill with ampacity: a conduit that is not overfilled can still need derating for many current-carrying wires.

Frequently asked questions

What is conduit fill?

Conduit fill is the share of a conduit's inside area taken up by the wires in it. The NEC limits it so wires can be pulled without damage and can shed heat. The limit is 53% for one wire, 31% for two wires and 40% for three or more.

How many #12 wires fit in 1/2 inch EMT?

Nine THHN or THWN-2 #12 AWG conductors fit in 1/2 inch EMT at the 40% limit. A tenth pushes the fill over 40%, so 3/4 inch is needed.

Why is the limit only 31% for two wires?

Two wires of similar size tend to wedge together in the conduit, which makes pulling harder than a mix of three or more. The NEC therefore uses a lower limit for exactly two conductors.

Does this calculator work for other conduit types?

Not yet. It covers EMT (electrical metallic tubing) only, because each conduit type has a different internal area. For PVC, rigid or flexible conduit, use the area from NEC Chapter 9 Table 4 for that type.

Which wire insulation does it assume?

THHN and THWN-2, the most common building wire. XHHW, RHW and other insulations have different areas, so a bundle of those can fill differently.

Do grounding wires count?

Yes. Every conductor inside the conduit counts toward fill, including equipment grounding conductors, so add them as wires of their own size.
